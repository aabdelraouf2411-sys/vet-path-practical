Digestive system

10 slides, 7 jars, 7 activities
Sections 1 and 2, plus activities from Section 3

Inflammation names
Enteritis: intestine. Colitis: colon. Typhlitis: cecum. Proctitis: rectum.
Causes of necrotic enteritis
Severe irritation, chemicals, bacteria (Salmonella), virus (Rinderpest), protozoa (Coccidiosis), vitamin B deficiency.
Granulomatous enteritis
A chronic inflammation characterized by granuloma formation. Causative agent: hyphae (Mucor).
True cirrhosis
Chronic toxic non-suppurative hepatitis with fibrosis, degeneration and regeneration.
Hepatic telangiectasis
Dilatation of blood vessels, pooling of blood, stagnation, disintegration of RBCs, then hemosiderin.

Sections 3 and 4

Emphysema
Over-distension of alveoli with air. Classified as acute or chronic, focal or diffuse, alveolar or interstitial.
Emphysema types
Acute general: young calves, allergic origin. Chronic diffuse: horses (broken wind or heaves). Acute or chronic focal: bronchitis with exudate and partial bronchial obstruction.
Atelectasis
A complete or incomplete air-less state of alveoli, from internal obstruction or external compression.
Pneumonia classified by
Distribution: lobar, lobular, interstitial. Agent: viral, parasitic, mycotic, gangrenous. Exudate: catarrhal, fibrinous, suppurative.

Easily confused

Ulcer or erosion

An ulcer is deep and destroys the basement membrane. An erosion is superficial and leaves the basement membrane intact.

True or Glissonian cirrhosis

True cirrhosis has fibrosis, degeneration and regeneration. Glissonian cirrhosis stays under the capsule, away from the main liver structure, and the nearby hepatocytes show pressure atrophy.

Multilobular or biliary cirrhosis

Multilobular shows nodules of regenerating hepatocytes with no central vein. Biliary shows flukes in the bile duct, portal fibrosis and newly formed bile ductules.

Fibrinous or catarrhal pneumonia

Fibrinous fills alveoli with a dirty pink fibrin network. Catarrhal shows goblet cell metaplasia and bluish mucus in the bronchial lumen.

Congenital or acquired atelectasis

In congenital atelectasis the alveoli are lined with cuboidal pneumocytes. In acquired atelectasis they are lined with flattened pneumocytes.

Acute or organized pericarditis

Acute shows serous exudate with macrophages and lymphocytes in a thick epicardium. Organized shows granulation tissue between normal muscle and fibrin meshes.

Productive or caseous mastitis

Productive has epithelioid cells and giant cells with no caseation or calcification. Diffuse caseous has homogeneous caseated material with calcification.
